PCI-focused safeguards in Truenote

Software controls, payment-data limits, and the assessment and operating evidence required before Truenote is introduced into an organization's PCI DSS environment.

Plain-language context

PCI DSS includes secure-software requirements and broader controls for systems that handle payment data or can affect its security. Truenote's software evidence supports a security review; customer scope, operating effectiveness, and validation remain unresolved. No PCI DSS compliance, certification, Attestation of Compliance, or independent-assessment result is claimed here.

PCI DSS v4.0.1 is the review basis. Requirements with a March 31, 2025 effective date are already effective. The compliance-accepting entity and qualified assessor determine applicability and the accepted validation path. See the PCI SSC document library and applicability guidance, checked October 9, 2026.

Payment-data boundary and scope

Truenote provides cited answers from approved reference material. No approved path for live payment-card data has been recorded. Until Security and the PCI scope owner approve a different assessed path, do not enter or upload primary account numbers (PAN), CVV/CVC, PIN or PIN blocks, full track data, payment screenshots, call transcripts containing payment data, credentials, or live customer personal data.

For the merchant/customer use being considered, sensitive authentication data must not be retained after authorization, even if encrypted. Truenote is not a payment vault or payment-capture channel. See PCI SSC guidance on sensitive authentication data.

Questions and model output

The ask route blocks supported secret, Social Security number, and card-number patterns in the current question and supplied history before its persistence and provider work. Text-provider firewalls add deterministic redaction, and Truenote's OpenRouter guardrail is configured to redact card numbers, Social Security numbers, contact details, names, and addresses in user messages on answer requests. Generated-answer screening covers a narrower pattern set.

Card-number detection requires 13-to-19-digit Luhn-valid values with space or hyphen separators. Pattern screening does not reliably identify standalone CVV/CVC, names, addresses, all credentials, or encoded and fragmented values. Personal notes, label names, and document titles are stored without these sensitive-content checks. A prohibited-data policy still needs technical enforcement, user training, and incident handling.

Documents and raw bytes

Object storage receives originals before ingestion. An enabled malware scanner receives raw bytes; PDF/image parsing can send them to LandingAI before parsed-content screening. Parsed text is persisted before its screening decision. Quarantining a version does not remove those copies or reverse the disclosure.

Only approved, sanitized source files should enter this path. Every raw-file processor, storage location, and recovery copy needs a scope and retention decision.

Scope and shared responsibility

A PAN prohibition or redactor alone does not establish that Truenote is outside PCI scope. Administrative access, connected systems, endpoints, hosting, providers, and the ability to affect the customer's cardholder data environment also require assessment.

Program and clearance isolation are application authorization controls. They do not prove PCI network segmentation. Provider certifications do not transfer compliance to Truenote or its customer.

Required scope records: data flows and inventories, scope and PAN decision procedure, and third-party responsibility matrix. Scope includes originals, parsed documents, questions and answers, audit/diagnostic records, backups, and provider copies.

Specific applicability decisions include automated public-facing web protection (6.4.2), payment-page script authorization/integrity/inventory (6.4.3), and payment-page change detection (11.6.1). CSP and origin checks alone do not establish those controls. Record an assessed justification for every exclusion; do not infer an exclusion from the application's intended use.

For applicable access, 8.2.8 requires reauthentication after 15 minutes of inactivity. Truenote currently has a seven-day application session lifetime without an enforced application idle timeout. The assessor must confirm whether a verified endpoint/session control satisfies the requirement or application changes are required. See PCI SSC idle-session guidance.

Requirements before security approval

Readiness remains incomplete. The demo/testing deployment intentionally leaves OIDC identity integration and the malware-scanner endpoint unconfigured; their integrations need end-to-end acceptance before customer use. SIEM additionally lacks database functions and triggers, so setting a receiver alone is insufficient. Public demo login remains configured. Current operating records also leave recovery, provider assurance, scope, and independent testing unresolved.

  1. Approve the boundary and permitted data. Name the application operator, customer PCI owner, and decision authority; inventory data, processors, administrative connections, storage, and recovery copies. Authorize a bounded synthetic trace, reconcile it, and approve the final scope and validation path.
  2. Establish safe operation. Resolve identity/MFA, session and privileged access, demo isolation, scanning and overrides, least-privilege database controls, external audit delivery, encryption, retention, and tested backup/restore. Create an authorized test environment or approve a documented substitute.
  3. Close release-management evidence. Reconcile current scanner results to the historical baseline, assign owners and deadlines, approve each disposition, retain remediation/retest, and pass the strict managed-release gate. Adopt change control and retain a reviewed production-change sample bound to deployed bytes.
  4. Obtain provider and independent assurance. Verify exact provider services, contracts, retention/ZDR, subprocessors, and applicable attestations. Complete authorized application/API, AI, and any applicable network/segmentation tests, then remediate and retest findings.
  5. Retain approval and recurring evidence. Authenticate signed decisions and assessment results, complete training and incident/recovery exercises, assign operating cadences and expiry reviews, and obtain customer security authorization before live organizational use.

Each requirement needs a named accountable person, a dated artifact tied to the environment and release, a binary acceptance result, and a reviewer. Templates, screenshots, structural validators, and passing CI alone cannot supply that evidence. The decision meeting pack and independent-testing plan define the next approval and assurance steps.

Assessment boundary: this page is a readiness and evidence summary. It does not establish PCI DSS compliance, certify software, or replace the customer's assessment. Assessment records must state the services, requirements, environment, and period actually tested, including exclusions and unresolved findings.
