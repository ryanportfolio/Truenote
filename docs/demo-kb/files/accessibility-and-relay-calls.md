# Accessibility and Relay Calls

Larkspur Cloud Customer Support · Guide CS-GDE-028 · Version 2 · Effective September 1, 2026 · Owner: Support Operations

## When to use this

Use this guide when a call comes through a relay service (a relay operator, captioned telephone or video relay interpreter), when a customer tells you they have a disability that affects how they use the phone or the app, or when a customer asks for documents in another format.

## Relay calls

Customers who are deaf, hard of hearing or speech-disabled may call through a Telecommunications Relay Service (TRS), often by dialing 711, or through Video Relay Service (VRS) with a sign language interpreter. The relay operator or interpreter is a conduit, not a third party.

- Speak to the customer, not the operator. Say "you", not "tell her".
- The operator types or signs everything you say. Pause after each sentence and avoid talking over the operator.
- The operator says "go ahead" (or "GA") when it is your turn. Do the same at the end of each of your turns.
- Never hang up on a relay call because of a pause or an automated announcement at the start. Relay calls often begin with the operator explaining the service.
- Do not ask the operator for their name or for a transcript. Relay services keep calls confidential.

## Verification on relay calls

Verification works the same as on any call: two factors from the Identity Verification procedure. The operator relays the customer's answers; that does not make the operator an unverified third party. Never ask for the full card number, the CVV or the password, on a relay call or any other.

The one-time code is often the easiest factor on a relay call, because the customer can read it from their own screen.

## Steps for a relay call

1. Read the required opening from the Call Scripts as usual, then say "go ahead".
2. Allow extra time for each answer. Do not treat a slow reply as a failed verification.
3. Keep sentences short and ask one question at a time.
4. Before a hold, ask permission as usual and say how long it will take. Check back at least every 2 minutes.
5. If the call will run past 6 minutes of hold time in total, offer a callback through the same relay service or an email follow-up instead.
6. Close with the required closing line and "go ahead", and wait for the customer to finish.
7. Add an account note that the call came through relay, so the next agent knows.

## Other accommodations

| Customer need | What you can offer |
|---|---|
| Prefers not to use the phone | Email support; the customer can also reply to any support email |
| Screen reader user stuck in the app | Read the exact button names; they are listed in the Help Center accessibility page |
| Needs more time on the call | Take it. Calls with an accessibility note are not counted against handle time |
| Large print or accessible PDF invoices | Billing Operations sends them; open a ticket |
| Customer asks you to slow down or repeat | Do so without comment |

## Words to use

Say "deaf" or "hard of hearing", "disabled" or "person with a disability", and "relay call". Avoid "hearing impaired" unless the customer uses it. Never comment on the customer's speech or ask about their disability; only ask what would make the call easier.

## Escalation

If a customer says the app or a support channel is not accessible to them, open a ticket for Support Operations with the details, in addition to helping with the original request. Complaints about discrimination go to a Tier 2 supervisor within 15 minutes.
