# Stage 7: Exit

The user is asking: can I say no, go back or stop, and what happens to my things if I do?

Covers every way out: "Not now" on an upsell, back and close, leaving a pricing page, cancelling or downgrading a plan. Exit is a stage of every journey, not only the last one: check it wherever a prompt or page asks for money. Hand-off rules and severity: [journey.md](../journey.md).

## Hand-off in and out

- **In**: the way out is visible at the same moment as the way in.
- **Out**: the user lands where they were before the prompt or page, with their task, choices and data intact (JO1). A decline that dumps them on the home page or clears their work is Blocks.

## Rules

### Applies when: a screen asks for payment, an upgrade or an add-on

**EX1. A decline of equal weight.** A modal, interstitial or inline upsell that interrupts a task has a plain "Not now" or "No thanks", visible without scrolling, neutral in wording, that returns to the task. A pricing page the user chose to open needs an unobstructed way back: navigation that works, and no overlay or exit prompt that blocks leaving. The video's complaint that "Maybe later" is too easy (2TlIg3VokY8 8:51) is rejected. Craft.

**EX2. Loss framing, honest version.** Keep: a real loss of the user's own data, with the date and the items affected. Remove: threat styling beyond the facts and dismiss copy that shames. Example: "Your storage is full. On May 3 we will delete the 14 oldest files (2.8 GB). Upgrade · Free up space · Not now". The same applies to a cancel flow that lists what the user will lose: real items and dates only. 2TlIg3VokY8 8:19. Adjacent research.

### Applies when: the product has subscriptions, plans or saved accounts

**EX3. A cancel route as short as the sign-up.** From the account area, find cancel or downgrade and count its steps against sign-up (JO2). The route says when access ends and what happens to the user's data, and ends with a confirmation. A retention offer, if any, is shown once, with an equal-weight way to continue cancelling (EX1). For app-store subscriptions the route is the phone's subscription settings; the app says so, and links there where the platform allows. Convention.

## Honesty items here

HO1 (confirmshaming dismiss copy), HO3 (cancel or downgrade harder than sign-up), HO10 (a dismiss styled to be missed), HO13 (a stock alert or "notify me" that also signs the user up for promotions). Details in [honesty.md](../honesty.md).

## Build moves

- Give every upsell and prompt a decline of equal weight that returns to the task (EX1).
- Write loss copy from real facts: date, items, size, and a way out that is not "Upgrade" (EX2).
- Build cancel and downgrade alongside sign-up, with a step count no longer than sign-up's (EX3).
- Make back and close restore the previous unfinished step with its choices (JO1); a finished step stays finished (JO4).

## At limited depth

Read-only: upsell declines, back and close are observable. The cancel route usually needs an account; read it from public help pages and mark it read, not observed, or list it under "Not checked". Never cancel a real subscription to check.
